section: Poster
abstract: 'Educational AI agents are usually introduced as tutors, homework helpers,
  or study companions. As children use them regularly, learning help can become a
  disclosure site: a student may begin with a math problem and then describe bullying,
  fear of adult reaction, or school avoidance. This position paper asks how child-facing
  educational agents should preserve student trust while deciding what stays private,
  what can be shared under student control, and what must be routed to accountable
  adults. Full parental visibility would turn help-seeking into surveillance; full
  secrecy would leave serious harm unsupported. We argue for bounded confidentiality
  as a communication-governance framework: ordinary tutoring remains private unless
  student choice or credible danger creates a reason to involve adults. The paper
  specifies a response ladder for minimal records, student-reviewed communication,
  care-oriented adult prompts, and escalation to locally accountable humans.'
